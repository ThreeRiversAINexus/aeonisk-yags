# Aeonisk

**Will is Power. Bond is Law. Void is Real.**

A science-fantasy roleplaying game about contractual magic, ledgered souls, and
eight factions with no clean hands.

---

## What Aeonisk is

Civilization runs under the **Sovereign Nexus**, and at its heart is the
**Codex** — an astral computer that judges every soul's actions. It does not
record events. It *tastes* them: in the moment an act occurs it reads the
emotional resonance of that act — who stood where, when, and what their souls
felt — and writes a verdict into a permanent ledger.

That verdict is **Soulcredit**, running from −10 to +10. High Soulcredit opens
doors: licensed magic, trusted standing, sanctioned Void work. Low Soulcredit
closes them, gate by gate. At −6 you are Cut Off, an exile in all but chains.
At −8 the prisons may take you. At −10 you may be lawfully hunted.

Magic works, it is everywhere, and it is *priced*. Every rite wants a primary
tool and a consumed offering, and must at least pay lip service to the Nexus to
be legitimate. The economy runs on crystallized elemental currency — Breath,
Drip, Grain, Spark — plus the illegal fifth, **Hollows**, which are story
complications first and money second.

Almost everyone is **made**: citizens gestate in Biocreche Pods under a Matron
Bond and emerge at a public Rite of Unveiling with their first Soulcredit
imprint already written. Natural birth is the Freeborn's rare, stigmatized
exception.

## What makes it different

Plenty of games have magic and corporations. These are the things Aeonisk does
that you will not find stacked together elsewhere.

**The magic has a legal department.** The law is not flavor text. The
*Sovereign Nexus Constitution* is a real, ratified statute included in this
packet — smuggling, self-defense, entrapment, the Void doctrine, moral luck —
and the Codex does not consult it. The Codex **is** that law, running. When you
break it, something reads the act and writes a number.

**Your Soulcredit is private until someone tests it.** Nobody reads your score
by looking at you. A vendor, a checkpoint, a contract-bound weapon — those find
out, and they learn your *standing*, never your deed. So the door closes and
no one is sent. The fall is real and it waits.

**The Codex's memory is imperfect, on purpose.** It keeps resonance — presence
and feeling at a time and place — but never a replay, so the past can always be
argued. A crime whose harm sleeps writes nothing until it **ripens**: poison in
the ground, a fraud undiscovered. When it surfaces, the presence-record becomes
the suspect pool. Every ripened crime is a locked room where the Codex holds
only the guest list. This is the setting's engine of mystery, not a hole in it.

**Bonds are mechanical, and scarce.** You may hold three. They are formal,
witnessed soul-connections, and they pay: bonded souls ritual better and defend
each other harder. In extremity a Bond can be sacrificed for terrible power.
Party cohesion is a rule here, not a seating arrangement.

**The Void is the price of power without connection.** Corruption from 0 to 10,
gained through unethical ritual, skipped offerings, and broken oaths. At 5+ it
warps reality around you. At 10 it takes you.

**Nobody is the villain.** There is no Empire and no Rebellion. The government
reading your soul genuinely believes it is the only thing holding civilization
together. The corporation that owns your debt is not lying about the terms. The
rebels selling Void contraband have the most honest account of what the system
costs, and they will still get you killed. Every faction has a case. You pick a
side knowing that.

## Who it's for

**You'll like this if you want:** consequences that are written down and come
back later; magic that costs something specific; politics where you can't
retreat to "well, they're the bad guys"; a rules layer light enough to run
conversationally but real enough that competence matters.

**Especially if you've wanted a Shadowrun** that took its metaphysics as
seriously as its gear tables — where the corp you're running against can
produce the paperwork proving you consented.

**Probably not for you if** you want clean heroic fantasy, a tactical
miniatures game, or a setting where the moral answer is legible up front.

**No experience needed.** You do not need to know YAGS, or any RPG. If you're
playing with an AI, you can start knowing nothing at all — read the next
section and you'll be playing in about two minutes.

## How to play, starting now

**Aeonisk is built to be run by an AI, and that is the intended way to play.**
No group to schedule, no session zero, nobody has to read sixty pages first.

1. Open any modern AI chat — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or anything else that
   takes file uploads and holds a long conversation.
2. **Upload the markdown files from the `ai/` folder.** They are plain text and
   deliberately flat: drag in the whole folder, or paste the files into the
   chat one after another. Either works.
3. **Add `AEONISK_SYSTEM_PROMPT.md`** (in the root of this packet). Paste it as
   your first message, or drop it into a system prompt / custom instructions /
   project instructions box if your tool has one. That file is what turns a
   general assistant into this game's narrator.
4. Send it. You'll get a short menu. If you know nothing about Aeonisk, pick
   **"Quickstart — I don't know anything. Let's play."**

That's the whole setup.

### In a hurry, or short on context?

Upload these five and add the rest later:

- `AEONISK_PRIMER.md`
- `Aeonisk - System Neutral Lore - v1.4.0.md`
- `Aeonisk - YAGS Module - v1.4.0.md`
- `NEXUS_LAW.md`
- `FACTION_REFERENCE.md`

Add Economy, Gear and Culinary when you want shopping, loadouts, or a scene in
a restaurant to have real numbers behind it.

### What the AI will actually do

The system prompt makes it run as the **Codex Nexum** rather than a generic
narrator. Concretely, it will roll in the open and show you every
`Attribute × Skill + d20`; track your Soulcredit and Void as they move; hold
you to the eight canon factions; and say *"I don't know"* instead of inventing
canon. That last one matters more than it sounds — the Codex's own memory is
lossy by design, so an honest gap in the record is in-genre rather than a
broken illusion.

It is still an AI. It will occasionally lose track of a number or contradict a
detail. Correct it and move on; the documents are the authority, and it is
instructed to defer to them.

## Playing at a table instead

It works with a human GM — it's a complete YAGS module and needs no computer.
One roll carries the game:

> **Attribute × Skill + d20**, versus a difficulty the GM sets from the stakes.

Eight attributes, skills rated 0–8. Unskilled attempts halve the die and fail
against anything hard, so competence is real and its absence is really felt.
Slow characters declare first and telegraph their intent; fast characters
declare last and act first, so speed buys information *and* tempo.

Before session one, the GM should know three things: **Soulcredit** (−10 to
+10, private until tested), **Void** (0 to 10, warps reality at 5+), and
**Bonds** (three at most, mechanically real, sacrificeable).

## House rules worth keeping

These are the things that go wrong first in a long campaign, AI-run or not.

- **Eight factions, never a ninth.** Sovereign Nexus, Pantheon Security, Astral
  Commerce Group, Arcane Genetics, House of Vox, Aether Dynamics, Tempest
  Industries, Freeborn.
- **ACG ≠ ArcGen.** Astral Commerce Group is debt. Arcane Genetics is biocreche.
- **The currency ladder is 20× per tier.** 1 Drip = 20 Breath, 1 Grain = 20
  Drip, 1 Spark = 20 Grain. Markets float ±20–30%; the ladder does not.
- **Soulcredit stays private** until something tests it.
- **Don't invent canonical families, events, artifacts, or institutions.** "I
  don't know" is a legitimate answer in this setting.
