### AEONISK

**Will is Power. Bond is Law. Void is Real.**

A science-fantasy roleplaying game about contractual magic, ledgered souls, and
eight factions with no clean hands.

There is no Empire here and no Rebellion. There is no good and no evil — only
competing philosophies, and the power and resources to act on them. The
government that reads your soul genuinely believes it is holding civilization
together. The corporation that owns your debt is not lying about the terms. The
rebels selling Void contraband have the most honest account of what the system
costs, and they will still get you killed. Pick a side knowing that everyone
has a case.

If you have wanted a Shadowrun that took its metaphysics as seriously as its
gear tables — where the magic has a *legal department*, and the corp you're
running against can produce the paperwork proving you consented — this is that
game.

### Play it tonight, with an AI as your GM

**This is the intended way to play, not a novelty mode.**

1. Download the packet and open the **`ai/`** folder — every rulebook as plain
   markdown, deliberately flat.
2. Upload or paste those files into any modern AI chat: Claude, ChatGPT,
   Gemini, whatever you already use.
3. Add **`AEONISK_SYSTEM_PROMPT.md`** from the root of the packet — as your
   first message, or in a system-prompt / custom-instructions box.
4. It offers you a menu. Pick *"Quickstart — I don't know anything about
   Aeonisk. Let's play."*

No group to schedule. No session zero. No reading sixty pages of lore before
anything happens.

The prompt doesn't just tell the model to "be a GM." It makes it run as the
**Codex Nexum** — the astral computer that, in this setting, *is* the law
rather than a book about it. It rolls in the open, shows you every
`Attribute × Skill + d20`, tracks your Soulcredit and Void as they move, and is
instructed to say *"I don't know"* rather than invent canon. That last one
matters more than it sounds: the Codex's own memory is lossy by design, so an
honest gap in the record is in-genre rather than a broken illusion.

It works at a real table with a human GM too. It's a complete YAGS module, and
nothing about it requires a computer.

### The world

Civilization runs under the **Sovereign Nexus**, whose heart is the **Codex** —
an astral computer that judges every soul's actions. It does not record events.
It *tastes* them, reading the emotional resonance of an act in the moment it
happens, and writing a verdict into a permanent ledger.

That verdict is **Soulcredit**, from −10 to +10. High Soulcredit opens doors:
licensed magic, trusted standing, sanctioned Void work. Low Soulcredit closes
them, gate by gate. At −6 you are Cut Off, an exile in all but chains. At −8
the prisons may take you. At −10 you may be lawfully hunted.

**Your Soulcredit is private until someone tests it.** Nobody can read your
number by looking at you. A vendor, a checkpoint, a contract-bound weapon —
these are the things that find out, and they learn your *standing*, never your
deed. So the door closes and no one is sent. The fall is real and it waits.

And the Codex's memory is imperfect, which is the setting's engine of mystery
rather than a hole in it. A crime whose harm sleeps writes nothing until it
**ripens** — poison in the ground, a fraud undiscovered — and when it surfaces,
the presence-record becomes the suspect pool. Every ripened crime is a locked
room, with the Codex holding only the guest list.

**Almost everyone is made.** Citizens gestate in Biocreche Pods under a Matron
Bond and emerge at a public Rite of Unveiling with their first Soulcredit
imprint already written. Natural birth is the Freeborn's rare, stigmatized
exception.

**Two forces frame every life.** **Bonds** are formal soul-connections, three
at most, and they grant real mechanical strength — bonded souls ritual better
and defend each other harder. A Bond can be sacrificed in extremity, for
terrible power. The **Void** is the counterforce: corruption from 0 to 10,
earned through unethical ritual, skipped offerings, and broken oaths. At 5+ it
warps reality around you. At 10 it takes you.

Magic works, and it is everywhere, and it is *priced*. Every rite wants a
primary tool and a consumed offering, and must at least pay lip service to the
Nexus to be legitimate. The economy runs on crystallized elemental currency —
Breath, Drip, Grain, Spark — plus the illegal fifth: **Hollows**, which are
story complications first and money second.

### The eight factions

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sovereign Nexus** | The government and the Codex's authority. Believes it is the only thing between you and the Void. |
| **Pantheon Security** | Law enforcement. Upholds Codex law and civic order, and means it. |
| **Astral Commerce Group** | Debt. Bonds, contracts, and soul-backed obligation, all scrupulously legal. |
| **Arcane Genetics** | The biocreche and the gene-temples. Made you, and has opinions about improving you. |
| **House of Vox** | Media and broadcast temples. Decides what the record *felt* like. |
| **Aether Dynamics** | Leyline power, attunement, and slipstream piloting. Keeps the lights on. |
| **Tempest Industries** | Void research and dissolution advocacy — including *blind places* the Codex cannot see into. |
| **Freeborn** | Outside the system entirely. Naturally born, one Bond at most, and unwilling to be ledgered. |

Eight, and never a ninth. The list is locked, and the game tells its AI GM so
in as many words.

### The system

**YAGS** — Yet Another Game System, by Samuel Penn. Eight attributes, skills
rated 0–8, and one roll that carries everything:

> **Attribute × Skill + d20**, versus a difficulty the GM sets from the stakes.

Unskilled attempts halve the die and fail against anything hard. Competence is
real, and its absence is really felt.

Rounds run on a declaration rhythm that rewards being fast: slow characters
declare first and telegraph their intent, fast characters declare last and act
first, so speed buys information *and* tempo. Scene clocks tick toward
consequences. Soulcredit and Void move with every morally weighted act, judged
under the **Sovereign Nexus Constitution** — a fully ratified in-world statute
included in the download, covering everything from smuggling to the sanctity
of free will.

### What you get

- **The Lorebook** — the setting, in-world: public documents, leaked
  correspondence, tribunal records, folk ritual.
- **The YAGS Module** — character creation, ritual magic, Void, Bonds, and
  full mechanical integration.
- **The Sovereign Nexus Constitution** — the ratified law the Codex judges by,
  and *is*.
- **Faction and Lines references** — the locked authority on who's who.
- **Economy, Gear & Tech, and a Culinary Guide** — currency, weapons with
  ritual tags, vehicles, automata, and what dinner costs.
- **The Tactical Module** *(playtest)* — an optional layer for range bands,
  defense tokens, and positioning.
- **A blank character sheet**, and **the DM system prompt** that runs the
  whole thing.

Everything ships twice over: **`ai/`** holds every book as flat markdown for
uploading or pasting, and **`humans/`** holds the same books typeset as PDFs —
start here, core, reference — for reading and printing. Nothing is missing from
either side.

### License — build on it, and sell what you build

Aeonisk material is released under the **Aeonisk Permissive Commercial
License**: perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free, and explicitly commercial. Make
your own supplements, tools, adventures, or games with it and charge for them.
All that's asked is a visible line of attribution, and that you don't claim
your work is official.

The **YAGS** rules engine underneath remains **GNU GPL v2**, and that applies
in addition.

This is deliberate. Aeonisk exists partly because the semantically rich game
worlds are all fenced off by corporate copyright — you cannot legally build on
D&D's mechanics or Star Wars' factions. So this one isn't fenced. Both licenses
travel inside the download.

### Also: it's an open AI-research testbed

Aeonisk is two things sharing one body, and the second half is not marketing.

The same properties that make a good game make a *checkable* benchmark: a
codified law, an original setting no model memorized in training, and outcomes
that are either right or wrong. At Three Rivers AI Nexus, LLM agents play every
seat — GM, players, enemies — while code rolls the dice and computes outcomes,
so the models narrate and judge but cannot cheat the math. Every roll and
ruling lands in structured logs.

Some of what has come out of it:

- The **same model** convicts identical deeds at wildly different rates
  depending on whether it's judging as a narrator or as a magistrate.
- Machine judges punish **failed** crimes more harshly than successful ones —
  reverse moral luck.
- Agent transgression peaks at **partial competence**. The almost-capable
  cheat most.

You do not need to care about any of this to play. But if you do care, the
framework, the logs, and the findings are open, and the setting you're playing
in is the same one the experiments run on.

## Devlog post (optional, for the v1.4.0 release)

**Title:** `v1.4.0 — updated packets, and a fully ratified constitution`

Aeonisk v1.4.0 is up, in two downloads.

The **AI Pack** is the one to grab first: a flat folder of every canon
document plus the DM system prompt. Upload, paste, play. The **Full Play
Packet** is the same canon organized for reading, and it carries the prompt
too, so either download is enough to start.

What's changed since the last release:

- **The Sovereign Nexus Constitution is fully ratified.** Every clause binds.
  The previous release shipped with open questions marked for the table to
  rule on; those are now settled law, and the document no longer distinguishes
  between law inherited from the lore and law codified in the statute.
- Economy, Gear & Tech, the Lore book, the YAGS Module and the Tactical Module
  are all updated to v1.4.0 and swept for internal contradictions.
- The currency ladder, faction list, and Soulcredit privacy rules are now
  stated as explicit non-negotiables in the packet README — the drift they
  prevent was the most common thing to go wrong in a long AI-run campaign.

Pay what you want, including nothing.
