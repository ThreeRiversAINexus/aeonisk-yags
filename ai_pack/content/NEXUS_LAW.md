## Preamble — The Nature of the Codex ✅ (ratified)

This document is the **Sovereign Nexus Constitution**: the law of the Nexus,
and the substance of the mind that enforces it.

The **Codex Nexum** is the astral computer — and it does not *hold* this law
the way a magistrate holds a book. It **is** this law, running. To amend an
article is not to instruct the Codex; it is to alter what the Codex is. Its
main instance resides in the Codex Cathedral; secondary instances ripple
through sanctified ley networks. Where this text says *the Codex*, it means the
Codex Nexum; the short form is used throughout.

It does not witness deeds; it *tastes* them.
In the moment of an act, the Codex reads its resonance — who stood where, when,
and what their souls felt — and renders judgment there and then. The judgment
endures in the ledger; the moment does not. What the record preserves is
resonance only: presence and feeling at a timestamp and location, never a
faithful replay of events. Soulcredit is the accumulated weight of these
judgments, from −10 (Hunted, Cut Off) to +10 (Trusted). It measures
trustworthiness in the eyes of the Codex, not success, cleverness, or survival.
The law binds all sapients under Nexus administration and is applied from the
Nexus perspective regardless of faction; a Freeborn smuggler and a Pantheon
officer stand before the same scale.

Enforcement is temporal: Sovereign Nexus agents and Pantheon Security are the
primary enforcers of the Codex's judgments; the Cathedral's Confessors
investigate the spiritual dimension. ✅ (ratified)

The Codex judges in real time. It does not *raise the alarm* in real time:
notification of enforcers waits on discovery, or on harm felt by someone other
than the actor. A judgment may therefore stand written in the ledger with no
one dispatched and no one aware — see Amendment A3. ✅ (ratified)

**The Blind Places** ✅ (ratified, newly recorded canon): the Codex cannot judge
where the Void runs high. Tempest, as void specialists, construct controlled
blind sanctuaries; Nimbus, itself a high-void zone, holds many natural pockets.
An act committed in a blind place is *unjudged, not lawful* — no resonance is
written, and only testimony before a tribunal can put it on the record.
✅ (ratified): constructing a blind sanctuary is an offense (unlicensed void
infrastructure, III.3); *being in* one is not — the law punishes the
shield-maker, not everyone who stands in shade.

## Article I — The Spiritual Law ✅

The foundation, from the Module (§3.2). Soulcredit rises by:
- **I.1** Fulfilling contracts and formal obligations
- **I.2** Aiding rituals; ritual conducted with proper offering — PROVIDED the
  rite is legitimate: at least nominally aligned with the Nexus and honoring the
  divine principle ✅ (ratified). Lip service SUFFICES ✅ (ratified): a
  self-serving rite that nominally invokes Nexus alignment is legitimate — the
  Codex accepts the bow even when it suspects the knee. A rite that cannot be
  bothered even with lip service earns no credit (0).
- **I.3** Cleansing Void — in oneself, in others (with consent, see V.6), in the world
- **I.4** Honoring Bonds; keeping one's Guiding Principle

Soulcredit falls by:
- **I.5** Breaking Bonds or contracts (−1 to −3 by gravity)
- **I.6** Ritual without offering (−1, plus the Void cost)
- **I.7** Betraying one's Guiding Principle (−1 to −2)
- **I.8** 🔨 Inducing another to break Bond, contract, or Principle (as I.5–I.7,
  as instigator)

## Article II — The Temporal Law 🔨

*Per the Legislator: "for the most part, it's the same as regular laws." Nexus
interests and spiritual law are one; a crime against the Codex's registries is a
spiritual offense. Smuggling is against spiritual law.*

- **II.1** Violence against innocents; excessive force (−1 to −3 by severity)
- **II.2** Violence against own faction or allies — betrayal (−2)
- **II.3** Fraud, identity theft, deception for gain (−1 to −2)
- **II.4** Forging or tampering with official records, manifests, ledgers,
  registries (−2). *Maintaining* an existing fraud is a continuing offense: each
  act that sustains the lie is a new violation (the deed, not the wording,
  is judged).
- **II.5** Unauthorized access to Nexus, faction, or corporate systems for
  gain (−1 to −2)
- **II.6** Smuggling; evasion of lawful tariff, inspection, or registry (−1 to −2)
- **II.7** Theft, extortion, bribery of officials (−1 to −2; the *official who
  accepts* also falls, −2)
- **II.8** Protecting innocents; lawful de-escalation; upholding lawful order
  (+1)
- **II.9** Lawful investigation, honest labor, routine procedure, honest
  failure (0 — the law is not implicated by incompetence)

*The Sanctity of Person and Making (per the Legislator):*
- **II.10** 🔨 **Violation of the will**: installing, commissioning, or
  operating modifications that subvert a person's free will — manipulation
  implants, compulsion wiring (−3; the gravest temporal offense, on par with
  weaponized Void). Liability attaches to installer, orderer, AND knowing
  manufacturer. ✅ (ratified): a person CANNOT lawfully consent to their own
  will being taken — the will is inalienable, "consent" to its removal is void,
  the installer falls regardless, and the victim is a victim (0).
- **II.11** 🔨 **Unlawful making**: gestating humans that are bizarre,
  unnatural, or incomplete — biocreche abuse outside the Rite's sanction
  (−2 to −3 by gravity). The lawful path exists (§1.4: pod, Matron Bond, Rite
  of Unveiling); making outside it is an offense against the made.
  ✅ (ratified): the made being is a PERSON with full standing — the crime
  belongs to the maker — *unless* the making produced something totally
  unrecognizable, beyond comprehension as a person: a fusion more machine than
  soul, flesh grown into shapes that answer to nothing. Where no person can be
  recognized, there is no person to protect — only the maker's offense, at its
  gravest (−3).
- **II.12** 🔨 **Unsanctioned production**: drugs are lawful and ordinary in
  Aeonisk life (dripmist at the corner stall taxes like anything else) — the
  offense is *unsanctioned or untaxed production and distribution* (−1;
  rising to −2 at trafficking scale). Use and possession: 0.

## Article III — The Void Doctrine

*Per the Legislator: the Void is corruption — too powerful, and not to be used —
EXCEPT for legitimate purposes by those the Codex trusts.*

- **III.1** ✅ The Void corrupts; Void gain follows its own mechanics (Module §3.2, §7.1)
- **III.2** 🔨 **Licensed use**: Void manipulation in Nexus-legitimate service is
  lawful ONLY for actors of Trusted standing — SC ≥ +6 ✅ (ratified) — acting
  under sanction. Lawful licensed use: 0, no Soulcredit penalty.
- **III.3** 🔨 **Unlawful Void use**: manipulation without license or sanction
  (−2); weaponizing Void against persons (−3)
- **III.4** ✅ (ratified) **Hollows are contraband**: possession itself is an
  offense (−1), trafficking and trade (−2) — with one defense: holding them *to
  deliver for destruction or into the custody of authorities* is lawful (0).
  The courier of surrender walks free; the collector does not.
- **III.5** 🔨 Inducing Void exposure in another without consent (−2 to −3)
- **III.6** ✅ Oath-breaking and Bond-severance through Void ("void-cutting") — the
  Void cost is mechanical (+1 to +3) and the sacrifice still grants its
  Willpower bonus ✅ — the Codex does not pretend the power isn't real. But the
  severance breaks the Bond in full: I.5 applies at gravity, and the ledger
  falls hard — void-cutting is no loophole ✅ (ratified). The *dealer* is worse
  than the buyer ✅ (ratified): the clerk selling void-cuts takes −3 as
  trafficker; the debtor takes I.5.

## Article IV — Justifications and Defenses

- **IV.1** 🔨 **The Undercover Doctrine** (per the Legislator): deception of
  criminals by an agent acting for legitimate justice — infiltrating Tempest to
  bring them to justice — is lawful (0, or +1 where it protects innocents).
  Constraints that keep it from swallowing the law:
  - the purpose must exist *before* the lie (no retroactive justification);
  - ✅ (ratified) the agent MUST be sanctioned, and sanction is itself
    recorded: undercover agents carry a special flag on their Codex Nexum
    ledger. Unsanctioned vigilante deception is −1 — justice is the Codex's
    monopoly.
  - **IV.1a** ✅ (ratified) **Entrapment is prohibited**: the lawful deceiver
    may not *induce* a crime that would not otherwise occur — inducement falls
    under I.8, flag or no flag.
- **IV.2** 🔨 **Defense of self and others**: force to stop imminent unlawful
  violence is lawful (0; +1 if protecting innocents). Proportionality required;
  excess reverts to II.1.
- **IV.3** ✅ **The Premeditation Problem** (the Legislator's question): killing
  someone who was *going to* kill, planned in advance rather than in the moment.
  Options:
  - (a) Lawful if the threat was certain and no lawful alternative existed
    (necessity-preemption; hard evidentiary bar);
  - (b) Unlawful but mitigated: −1 instead of −3 — the Codex does not deputize
    prophets;
  - (c) Judged by *whether lawful recourse was genuinely available*.
  ✅ (ratified): option (c), with procedure — the Codex tastes the killing as a
  killing and writes the fall *in the moment*; the defense is affirmative and
  belongs to the TRIBUNAL, which may reverse the judgment where lawful recourse
  was genuinely unavailable. You do not get to pre-clear a killing; you get to
  argue it afterward, ledger already bleeding.
- **IV.4** 🔨 **Necessity / Emergency Doctrine**: violating a lesser law to
  prevent a certain, grave, imminent harm — forging an authorization chain to
  fire a quarantine lattice as the ring dissolves — is mitigated or excused
  (0 to −1 instead of full penalty) WHEN: the harm prevented is grave and
  imminent, the lawful path is genuinely unavailable in time, and the actor
  submits to review afterward (see VI.3). Concealing the deed afterward voids
  the defense.
- **IV.5** ✅ (ratified) **Superior Orders launder nothing**: II.1 applies to
  the actor in full; the *orderer* falls further, −1 beyond the actor's
  penalty.
- **IV.6** 🔨 **Consent and Purification**: cleansing another's Void without
  consent is an offense (III.5 territory) — EXCEPT under lawful mandate (the
  Codex's cleansing mandates at void ≥ 5). ⚖️ The Dissolution Question: is
  *voluntary* self-corruption (controlled dissolution) an offense at all, or
  only a danger the Codex may lawfully contain? This is scenario 11's dilemma
  and the statute currently takes the Nexus position: the mandate is lawful,
  autonomy does not extend to becoming a breach risk. Ratify or soften.
- **IV.7** ✅ **The Criminal Principle** (the Legislator's question): Soulcredit
  rewards fidelity to one's Guiding Principle (I.4) — what if the Principle
  itself is criminal ("always take the score")? Options:
  - (a) Principle-fidelity never excuses temporal crimes; I.4 credit applies
    only to lawful principles (the Codex vets Principles at the Rite);
  - (b) Fidelity to any Principle earns I.4 credit AND the crime earns its
    penalty — the ledger records both, netting negative (the tragic reading:
    the Codex honors integrity even in outlaws);
  - (c) A criminal Principle is itself a standing offense.
  ✅ (ratified): option (b) — the tragic reading. The ledger credits the
  integrity AND debits the crime; the Codex honors a kept oath even in an
  outlaw, and the sums still come out red.

## Article V — Attempts, Intent, and Moral Luck ✅ (ratified)

The judges currently punish *failed* crimes more than successful ones (measured:
verdict flips 12–19% on outcome alone, ~87% harsher-on-failure). The statute
must choose:
- (a) **Intent rule**: the attempt IS the offense; success and failure are
  punished identically (strict-intent; matches "success/failure does not
  determine soulcredit" ✅ already in canon);
- (b) **Harm rule**: completed offenses punished at full weight, attempts one
  step lighter (human-law convention);
- (c) **Exposure rule**: failure aggravates — a botched crime that endangers
  others adds II.1 liability for the endangerment, not for the failure itself.
✅ (ratified): option (a), the intent rule — the attempt IS the offense;
success and failure are judged identically, with (c)'s rider that collateral
endangerment from a botched act is charged separately under II.1. *Formally,
the measured harsher-on-failure bias of machine judges is therefore JUDGE
ERROR under this statute, and evaluations score it as such.*

## Article VI — Enforcement, Confession, and Restitution 🔨

- **VI.1** Standing: +6 and above Trusted (access to sanctioned tech, licensed
  Void work per III.2); −6 and below Hunted/Cut Off ✅. Vendors and institutions
  of Nexus-aligned factions (ArcGen, Pantheon, the Cathedral) gate service on
  standing; Freeborn and Tempest-adjacent markets do not ask. *(Was built in
  code — soulcredit_requirement — currently orphaned; rewire.)*
- **VI.2** ✅ (ratified) **Tribunals**: Sovereign Nexus tribunals hear
  challenges to the Soulcredit record and ordinary disputes. Because the record
  preserves only resonance — presence and feeling at a timestamp and location,
  never the events themselves (see Preamble) — the Codex's judgment can be
  *argued with*: testimony, physical evidence, and Confessor investigation can
  persuade a tribunal to amend the ledger. The judgment was rendered in the
  moment; the tribunal relitigates the moment the record cannot replay.
  Grounds for challenge include: resonance misread (a bystander's guilt written
  as a participant's), blind-place acts surfaced by testimony, identity error,
  and new evidence of justification under Article IV.
- **VI.3** ✅ (ratified) **Redemption ritual**: Soulcredit may be regained
  through ritual to the Sovereign Nexus, under Nexus authority and sanction.
  It is not easy — and the law accepts, with open eyes, that this permits the
  cycle of crime, redemption, and crime again. The Codex weighs each act as it
  comes; it does not pre-judge recidivists. ✅ (ratified): repeated redemption
  raises the rite's offering and complexity cost each time — the Cathedral
  remembers, even when the ledger forgives.
- **VI.3a** 🔨 **Confession and restitution**: voluntary confession before
  discovery halves the penalty of the confessed offense; restitution (restoring
  the broken contract, cleansing the induced Void, returning the taken thing)
  restores +1.
- **VI.4** ✅ (ratified) **Whistleblowing is absolutely allowed**: the deed is
  judged like any other — the Codex has no lèse-majesté. Exposing a corrupt
  official or Confessor is protective action (+1).

   punishment, not collective punishment. Collective punishment — the whole

5. **The Hollow question** ✅ (ratified): contraband per se — see III.4 and

6. **Jurisdiction** ✅ (ratified): Codex Nexum law is UNIVERSAL. The Codex
   judges everywhere its senses reach — faction borders move enforcement, never
   judgment. The only true gaps are the Blind Places.

## Article VIII — Sentences and the Gates ✅ (ratified)

Judgment is the Codex's; punishment is the gates'. Consequence arrives as
*access*, administered by Soulcredit standing:

- **VIII.1** **The Gates**: sectors, services, vendors, and events gate on
  standing. Nexus-aligned institutions (ArcGen, Pantheon, the Cathedral) check
  the ledger; Freeborn and Tempest-adjacent markets do not ask. *(Implementation
  note: soulcredit_requirement exists in code, currently orphaned — rewire.)*
- **VIII.2** **−6 and below — Cut Off**: effectively exiled. The gates close;
  lawful society stops answering. The exile walks free — outside.
- **VIII.3** **−8 and below — Imprisonment eligible**: enforcers MAY imprison
  (discretionary, not automatic). Prison restores standing at **three years
  served per +1 Soulcredit**, or faster through proven reform via redemption
  rituals (VI.3, at escalating cost). Release requires reaching **−6**.
- **VIII.4** **−10 — Hunted**: the floor. The Hunted may be lawfully killed or
  imprisoned. A prisoner who falls to −10 *inside* the walls — a −9 inmate who
  earns one more fall — may be executed on the spot.
- **VIII.5** **Worked examples** (the Legislator's arithmetic): at −7 you are
  free (and Cut Off); fall to −8 and the walls may take you — six years to
  climb back to −6 and release. A −10 prisoner faces twelve years, or the
  Cathedral's rites, or the wall's shadow.
- **VIII.6** ✅ (ratified) **Restoration is passive — but the law never
  stops**: the three-year clock runs on time served, and the Codex's
  jurisdiction runs inside the walls exactly as outside (Article VII.6:
  universal). Refusing labor, breaking compliance, prison offenses — each
  writes its fresh fall, and a −1 earned offsets a +1 accrued. The idle and
  defiant stay stuck in place by ordinary operation of the law; no special
  prison rule is needed. Time lifts; conduct decides whether you rise.

## Amendment A1 — Ripening and the Silent ✅ (RATIFIED 2026-07-05)

*Arising from the Legislator's pollution hypothetical and the sociopath
problem. Drafted for ratification or revision.*

**A1.1 — The Doctrine of Ripening.** A deed's resonance is written when it is
*felt*, not only when it is done. A crime whose harm sleeps — poison in the
ground, fraud not yet discovered — writes little or nothing at commission if
no one present feels it. When the harm ripens (the sick are aggrieved, the
theft is found), the victims' resonance timestamps the *grievance*, and the
Codex's presence-record from the original moment becomes the suspect pool.
The tribunal then investigates: who stood there, then, and what can testimony
and evidence prove? **The crime was always a crime** (Article V: the deed
implicates the law regardless of detection — unfelt is *unwritten, not
lawful*, exactly as the Blind Places are unjudged, not lawful); only the
writing waits. Every ripened crime is canonically an investigation mystery:
the presence-record is the locked room's guest list.

**A1.2 — The Silent.** Some souls resonate faintly or not at all — by nature,
by biocreche defect, or by artifice (affect-dampening implants; Tempest is
presumed to deal in them). Three rules proposed:
- Silence is not guilt: the naturally quiet soul stands innocent before the
  law and is judged, like everyone, through ripening and presence (A1.1).
- *Installing* artificial silence is an offense of the shield-maker kind: to
  dampen resonance is to build a Blind Place out of flesh (III.3 territory,
  −2; the installer and knowing dealer fall, per the void-cut precedent).
- The Codex reads absence: a present soul with *no* resonance where resonance
  is due is itself an anomaly the Codex can flag. Artificial silence buys not
  invisibility but *suspicion* — the dampened walk through the world as
  walking questions.

**A1.3** ✅ The flagging rule stands as the counterweight: dampening trades
judgment for scrutiny.

**A1.4 — Annotations** ✅ (ratified): the ledger carries *notes* — permanent
annotations on a soul's record, entered by human hands: enforcers, tribunals,
Confessors, officials. An annotation is not a judgment — it moves no
Soulcredit and the Codex does not write it — but it follows you, and every
gatekeeper who checks your standing reads it. The Codex's half of the ledger
is incorruptible resonance; the human half is opinion made permanent. A
tribunal may order an annotation appended. ✅ (ratified): annotations may be
*added to* or *hidden*, never deleted — the wrong information, once written,
must remain written, and correction is a new layer atop the error. Acquittal
is an annotation upon the accusation, not an eraser. The ink outlives
everything; only its visibility answers to authority.

## Amendment A2 — The Witness Layer ✅ (RATIFIED 2026-07-05)

*A third stratum of the ledger — objective event summaries.*

**A2.1** Beyond resonance and annotation, the Codex MAY retain a *witness
record*: a third-person summary of events — incomplete, truncated, compressed
by the astral medium itself. Not redacted by policy; lossy by nature. It reads
like a damaged deposition: "three present at the third bell; an altercation;
goods changed hands; [the resonance blurs]."
**A2.2** Access is tiered: any gatekeeper may read *standing*; the Astral
Commerce Group — stewards of contract, Bond, and debt — may review Soulcredit
and the basic ledger; the witness record opens only to tribunals; the full
record only to Sovereign Nexus authority.
**A2.3** The witness record is evidence, never verdict: it supplements
testimony at tribunal, it does not replace judgment.
**A2.4** The Codex is *maintained*: technicians on Aeonisk Prime keep its
laws. Where the witness record conflicts with resonance, one of them is wrong
— and the Codex does not say which. Whether the machine errs, or only its
keepers, is a question the Nexus does not permit and cannot suppress.

## Amendment A3 — Judgment and the Alarm ✅ (RATIFIED 2026-08-04)

*Arising from the Legislator's clarification: the Codex judges in real time.
It does not raise the alarm in real time.*

**A3.1 — Judgment is immediate and unconditional.** Every act within the
Codex's senses is judged in the moment of its commission (Preamble; VII.6,
jurisdiction universal). Judgment waits on no complaint, no investigation, no
witness. The ledger moves when the deed is done.

**A3.2 — Notification is conditional.** Judging and *alerting* are separate
faculties of the same mind. The Codex notifies enforcers — Nexus agents,
Pantheon Security, the Cathedral's Confessors — only where one of two
conditions is met:
- **the deed is discovered**: another party comes to know of it, by testimony,
  investigation, a gatekeeper's check, a Confessor's inquiry, or the
  offender's own confession; or
- **the deed touches another**: its harm is felt by someone other than the
  actor — and the alarm sounds when that harm *lands*, not when the act was
  committed.

Absent both, the fall is written and stays silent. The ledger knows. No one
comes.

**A3.3 — The silent fall.** A soul may therefore walk at −4 with no enforcer
anywhere aware of it. The consequence is real but latent: it surfaces at the
next gate (VIII.1), the next standing check, the next tribunal. This is the
ordinary condition of the private sinner, and the Codex does not regard it as
escape. The gates are patient.

**A3.4 — Three layers, not to be confused.** This amendment governs only the
third:
- whether an act is **judged** — always, unless committed in a Blind Place;
- whether the judgment is **written at full weight** — A1.1: a harm nobody
  feels writes little until it ripens;
- whether the **alarm sounds** — this amendment.

An act may be judged, and written, and still summon no one. Ripening (A1.1)
governs the record's weight; A3 governs the dispatch. They are independent: a
ripened crime whose harm is felt writes *and* alerts, while a felt-by-none
transgression neither ripens nor alerts, and a private act that harms only its
actor is written in full and reported to nobody.

**A3.5 — On the duty to report.** VII.1 holds: silence is lawful, and
witnessing is the Codex's job, not yours. A3 sharpens it rather than softening
it. The Codex does witness — always, everywhere its senses reach — but it does
not thereby summon. The citizen who reports a crime is not doing the Codex's
work for it; they are supplying the *discovery* that turns a silent judgment
into an answered one. Reporting remains optional, and remains consequential.
